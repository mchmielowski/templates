Finding light: a guide to natural-light portraiture

Window light is the most generous tool in a photographer's kit — and the most overlooked. Here is how we shape it on every Foll shoot.

The best portrait light is usually the one already in the room. Before we reach for a single strobe, we read the windows: their direction, their height, and the way the day moves across them. North-facing glass gives that even, shadowless wash editorial clients love; a low west window an hour before sunset turns skin warm and sculpts cheekbones with almost no effort. Learning to see those differences is most of the job.

"Don't fight the light you have — learn its mood and build the frame around it."

Once you can read a room, shaping the light is simple. A sheer curtain softens a harsh midday beam into something flattering; a sheet of white foamcore opposite the window bounces just enough fill to open up the shadows without flattening the face. Position your subject so the light comes in at an angle rather than head-on, watch for the catchlight in their eyes, and shoot wide open to let the background fall away. No power outlets, no light stands — just a window and a little patience.
