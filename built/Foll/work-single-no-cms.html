3098 Cinemas

3098 Cinemas asked Foll to reimagine a beloved independent movie house for a younger audience without losing the charm that kept regulars coming back for decades.

We started in the lobby with a camera and a notebook, mapping how people actually move through the space — buying tickets, queuing for popcorn, finding their screen. That research shaped a new identity built around a flexible marquee system: a bold display typeface, a warm amber-and-charcoal palette, and a set of pictograms that read instantly from across the room. The goal was a brand that felt cinematic in motion as well as in print.

‍

From there we rolled the system out across every touchpoint — animated showtime boards, printed programs, tickets, tote bags and a refreshed website. A full photography shoot captured the projection booth, the velvet seats and the staff who run the place, giving the brand a sense of people and place rather than stock imagery. Three months after launch, weekday attendance was up noticeably and the new visual language had become a recognised part of the neighborhood again.

“Foll didn’t just give us a logo — they gave the whole cinema a voice. People notice the marquee from the street now, and that’s brought a new crowd through the doors.”

The marquee was the heart of the project. We rebuilt it as a modular grid so staff could swap titles in minutes, then designed a motion kit that brings the same letterforms to life on the lobby screens and online. Print and digital now share one system, which means a new release looks consistent the moment it goes up — on the wall, on the site and across social.

‍

We also gave 3098 Cinemas a small but flexible toolkit for everything beyond the screen: ticket stubs designed to be kept, a members program with its own card, seasonal posters for festival weeks, and a merch line built from the core marks. Each piece reuses the same handful of elements, so the brand stays recognisable while leaving plenty of room for the team to keep it fresh season after season.
