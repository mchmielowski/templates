How I Plan a Month of Content in a Single Afternoon

For the first two years of creating, I made content the way most people do — frantically, the night before, fueled by caffeine and low-grade panic. It worked, sort of, but it left me exhausted and the quality was all over the place. Then I started batching, and everything changed.

Now I block off one afternoon a month, brew a pot of coffee, and map out the next four weeks in a single sitting. By the time I close my notebook, every post, reel, and newsletter has a topic, a hook, and a rough outline. The rest of the month becomes execution instead of invention — and that shift is everything.

I start by pulling the three or four themes my audience keeps returning to: creative routines, behind-the-scenes process, and the occasional honest post about burnout. From there I sketch a simple grid — one anchor piece per week, supported by lighter posts that pull from the same idea. Repurposing one strong concept into five formats is far easier than inventing five separate ideas.

The last step is the one people skip: I schedule a recharge day. A month of content means nothing if the person making it runs out of things to say. Protect the input, and the output takes care of itself.
