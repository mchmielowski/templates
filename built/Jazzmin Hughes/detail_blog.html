Every creative project begins long before a single mark is made. It starts in the quiet moments — walking home at dusk, flipping through a worn reference book, or hearing a chord that refuses to leave your head. For me, the spark usually arrives uninvited, and the work is simply learning to catch it before it disappears. I keep a running notes file on my phone, a sketchbook on my desk, and a voice-memo folder full of half-finished ideas that I return to when inspiration runs dry. Most never make it to a final piece, but they all feed the ones that do. The process is less about waiting for a perfect idea and more about staying porous — remaining open to influence from art, music, architecture, and the ordinary textures of daily life.

Collaboration has shaped almost every piece in this body of work. Even projects that appear solitary were made richer by a well-timed conversation, a borrowed record, or a collaborator who pushed back at exactly the right moment. I have come to believe that creative isolation is a myth — we are always in dialogue with something or someone, whether we acknowledge it or not. Going forward, I plan to make that dialogue more intentional: residencies, open studios, and shared workspaces are all on the horizon. The most exciting work I can imagine is the work I have not yet been influenced enough to make.
